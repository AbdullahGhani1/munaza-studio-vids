# Neck pain film: storyboard variants

All three follow the brief's structure (hook, lock-in, big question, stakes, headfake, rehooks) and the 177 s timeline. They differ in how the world is staged.

## A. The Day on Pause (recommended, built)

A handcrafted 2.5D paper-cut world around Ayesha (32). Each interrupted moment gets a small red paper **pause tab** that pops up beside her. The tabs stand for interruption, not anatomy. Dark lit paper scenes open the film. The headfake card "ONE QUICK FIX?" folds into a consultation notebook, and the world turns light for care, practice and the website. It closes on a dark brand finish where paper fragments assemble into the logo.

- **Why A:** the pause tab is one simple, repeatable visual idea that viewers learn in 10 seconds. It carries the stakes without frightening imagery, and it pays off at the end: the tabs stop appearing, and she turns freely toward her mother.

## B. Small Movements, Meaningful Moments

An intimate, close-up film: hands on a keyboard, a turning page, a cup of tea, eyes glancing sideways. Wide shots are rare, and every scene is a still-life that comes alive when a small movement happens.

- **Trade-off:** emotionally strong, but the care journey (assessment → plan → review) is harder to show clearly in close-ups, and the website section would feel disconnected.

## C. A Day in Layers

One continuous camera move through miniature dioramas (bedroom → desk → living room → clinic → home again), like a pop-up book. Transitions are camera pushes through doorways and windows.

- **Trade-off:** very smooth, but a single continuous move is slow for vertical social viewing, and it leaves no room for hard cuts on rehook questions.
